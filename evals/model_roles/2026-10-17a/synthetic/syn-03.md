Is Orrery-Pro a sign that the frontier is flattening?

Lumenfield released Orrery-Pro last week, and the reaction has been strangely muted. I want to argue that this is the right reaction, but for the wrong reasons.

The headline claim is that Orrery-Pro reasons more reliably over long documents than Lumenfield's previous model. I read the release notes twice, and every comparison in them is against Lumenfield's own earlier systems. That is not nothing, but it tells us little about where Orrery-Pro sits against everyone else.

My actual worry is about the safety section. Orrery-Pro's release notes describe a new "deliberation budget" that lets the model think longer before answering risky requests. If that works, it is a real advance. But the notes give no numbers, only two hand-picked examples. Until someone outside Lumenfield measures how Orrery-Pro behaves when users push back on its refusals, I will treat the safety claims as marketing.

So: muted, yes. But not because Orrery-Pro is unimpressive. Because we have not been shown enough to know.
