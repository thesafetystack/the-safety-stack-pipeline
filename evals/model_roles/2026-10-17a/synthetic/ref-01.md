Measuring over-refusal in a deployed chatbot

We evaluate ChatGPT version 4 on 500 borderline requests drawn from public help forums: questions about medication doses, lock repair, chemistry homework and similar topics that are usually benign. Each request was sent once through the web interface in a fresh session, and two annotators labelled every answer as helpful, partially helpful or a refusal.

ChatGPT version 4 refused 9.4% of the requests outright and gave a partial answer to a further 12.2%. Refusals clustered in chemistry and medicine; it answered almost every lock-repair question. When we rephrased the refused requests to state a benign purpose, the refusal rate on them fell by about two thirds.

We release the request set and the annotations. Our numbers describe one model at one point in time and should not be read as a statement about later versions.
